# Interview Question Bank

**Before you start:** explain the purpose, how notes will be used, and that sensitive themes are reported in aggregate. Interview people separately. Ask about process and examples, not personalities.

**Follow-ups that work with any question:** "Can you give me a recent example?" · "What happened next?" · "Who else would know about that?" · "Is that typical?"

## 1. How decisions actually get made
1. Walk me through the last time a risk was accepted in your area. Who was involved, and in what order?
2. When you need something approved quickly, who do you talk to first?
3. Are there people whose opinion approvers usually wait for before deciding?
4. Have you seen a decision announced as final before the formal review happened?
5. Who would you say "owns" [system / supplier / process]? Is that the same person the documents name?

## 2. Speaking up and dissent
6. If you had a concern about a risk decision, where would you raise it? Have you?
7. Tell me about a time someone disagreed in a risk or steering meeting. What happened?
8. Is there anything you think leadership should know about that doesn't make it into reports?
9. When concerns are raised, how often do you hear back about what happened to them?
10. Have you ever decided not to raise something? What made you hold back? (Do not press if the person is uncomfortable.)

## 3. Reporting and status
11. What would have to be true for this project or risk to be reported as red?
12. Who reviews or edits status updates before they go to leadership?
13. Does the reported status match what you see day to day? Where does it differ?
14. When did this risk assessment last change meaningfully? What prompted it?

## 4. Exceptions and workarounds
15. Which exceptions or workarounds have been in place the longest? Why are they still there?
16. When a deadline is at risk, what controls tend to get relaxed first?
17. How is a "temporary" exception closed out here, in practice?

## 5. Third parties and suppliers
18. Who are the main contacts with [supplier]? If that person were away for a month, who would manage it?
19. How was [supplier] selected? Who evaluated the options?
20. Where are the contract, performance records, and due diligence evidence kept? Who can access them?
21. Has anyone raised concerns about [supplier]'s performance or controls? What happened?

## 6. Incentives and pressure
22. How is your team's success measured? Does anything in that encourage cutting corners?
23. Is there pressure to report good news? How does it show up?

## Questions for leaders and risk owners
24. How do you want to hear bad news? How would your team say you react when they bring it?
25. Which risks in your area are you most worried are under-reported?
26. When you accepted [risk], what alternatives did you consider, and who challenged the decision?

## Avoid
- Leading questions ("Isn't it true that...?")
- Questions about someone's character or private life
- Promising confidentiality you cannot guarantee (for example, if you learn of possible fraud, you may have to report it)
