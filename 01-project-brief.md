# 01 · Project Brief

## Objective

Give a GRC or third-party risk analyst a repeatable, fair way to identify **how power and behavior shape risk decisions**, and to turn qualitative observations into findings that are factual, proportionate, and defensible.

## The problem

Most risk programs are built to measure: scores, heat maps, KRIs, control test results. Those tools assume the inputs are honest and the decision process works as designed. In practice:

- The person with the most authority in the room often sets the risk rating, and others follow.
- Risk acceptances get signed by the same leader who owns the risk and benefits from accepting it.
- Concerns get raised informally and never written down.
- Status reports stay green because reporting red carries a personal cost.

None of this shows up in a score. It shows up in **language, meeting behavior, document trails, and relationships**, which an analyst can observe and evidence.

## Scope

**In scope**
- Formal vs. informal power in risk decisions (org chart vs. who actually decides)
- How authority shapes risk appetite, risk acceptance, and exception approval
- Judgment distortions in risk assessment (anchoring, optimism, groupthink, normalization of deviance, HiPPO effect, availability)
- Social dynamics in the control environment: override, intimidation, rubber-stamp approvals, fear of escalation
- Structures that create or hide risk: management override, conflicts of interest, siloed ownership, accountability gaps, watermelon reporting
- Third-party and supply chain relationships where one person controls the vendor

**Out of scope**
- Assessing individuals' character, personality, or intent
- Psychological profiling or reading single gestures as proof
- Covert monitoring of employees
- Numeric risk scoring models (covered in the [AI Supply Chain Risk Assessment](https://github.com/Starboy1968/ai-supply-chain-risk-assessment) project)

## Questions this project answers

1. Who actually influences risk decisions here, and does that match the formal authority?
2. Is risk acceptance happening at the right level, by someone independent enough to accept it?
3. What observable signs show that dissent is welcomed, tolerated, or punished?
4. Which words and documents suggest risk is being minimized or ownership hidden?
5. When does a green dashboard deserve a second look?
6. How do I grade qualitative evidence so a finding is not "just an opinion"?
7. How do I write and escalate a power-related finding safely, factually, and without making it personal?

## Ground rules used throughout

- **Patterns over moments.** One interruption is a moment. The same voice interrupted in four of five meetings is a pattern.
- **Process, not people.** Findings describe conditions, decisions, and effects on risk, not character.
- **Triangulate.** No finding rests on a single source.
- **Proportionate response.** Start with the lightest effective action; escalate only as evidence strengthens.
- **Privacy and fairness.** Observe work settings and work records only; protect interviewees; give management a chance to respond.
