## Real cases (briefly summarized, labeled as real)

These are cited only for the power and behavior patterns their official records describe. Summaries are kept short and stick to what the linked sources state.

### Real case: Space Shuttle Challenger (1986): normalization of deviance and overruled engineers
- **Pattern:** Sociologist Diane Vaughan described how NASA repeatedly accepted O-ring erosion evidence until it became normal, which she called the *normalization of deviance*. Testimony to the Presidential (Rogers) Commission describes engineers at the contractor objecting to the launch, a management-only caucus, a senior manager asking the engineering lead to shift from an engineering perspective to a management one, and engineers not being polled on the final recommendation.
- **Indicators illustrated:** A3, E2 (normalization of deviance); B1, B3, C1 (authority overriding expert dissent).
- **Sources:** [Report of the Presidential Commission, Vol. 1, Ch. 5 (NASA)](https://www.nasa.gov/history/rogersrep/v1ch5.htm) · [Vaughan, *The Challenger Launch Decision*, University of Chicago Press](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/C/bo22781921.html)

### Real case: Wells Fargo sales practices (2002 to 2016): incentives and pressure
- **Pattern:** In 2020, Wells Fargo agreed to pay $3 billion to resolve criminal and civil investigations. Per the U.S. Department of Justice, the Community Bank pressured employees to meet unrealistic sales goals, which led thousands of employees to open millions of accounts or products without customer consent or under false pretenses.
- **Indicators illustrated:** F1 (reporting that hides reality), incentive pressure (COSO 2013 Principle 5, excessive pressures).
- **Sources:** [U.S. DOJ press release, Feb 21, 2020](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/wells-fargo-agrees-pay-3-billion-resolve-criminal-and-civil-investigations-sales-practices) · [Statement of Facts (DOJ, PDF)](https://www.justice.gov/d9/press-releases/attachments/2020/02/21/wells_fargo_-_exhibit_a_-_statement_of_facts_0.pdf)

### Real case: Boeing 737 MAX (House committee report, 2020): production pressure and concealment
- **Pattern:** The House Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure's final majority staff report identified themes including production pressures, a "culture of concealment," conflicted representation in FAA oversight, and instances where FAA management overruled its own technical experts at Boeing's behest.
- **Indicators illustrated:** F1, D3 (influence over oversight), C1/C2 (expert concerns overruled), D4 (conflicted roles).
- **Sources:** [Committee press release, Sept 16, 2020](https://democrats-transportation.house.gov/news/press-releases/after-18-month-investigation-chairs-defazio-and-larsen-release-final-committee-report-on-boeing-737-max) · [Final report (PDF)](https://democrats-transportation.house.gov/imo/media/doc/2020.09.15%20FINAL%20737%20MAX%20Report%20for%20Public%20Release.pdf)

### Real case: Wirecard (2020): third-party arrangements and unverifiable balances
- **Pattern:** In June 2020, Wirecard's management board stated there was a prevailing likelihood that EUR 1.9 billion in trust account balances, linked to its third-party acquiring business, did not exist. Its auditor EY had refused to sign off on the 2019 accounts and said it had been given false confirmations. The company filed for insolvency days later.
- **Indicators illustrated:** D1/C4-type information gatekeeping around third parties; the importance of obtaining evidence directly from the source (Scenario 5).
- **Sources:** [Wirecard management board statement, June 22, 2020 (PDF)](https://wirecard.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/AH_2020_06_22_Statement-Board.pdf) · [Reuters, June 25, 2020](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/the-moneys-gone-wirecard-collapses-owing-4-billion-idUSL8N2E21UT/)

## Terms used without a specific citation
Anchoring, optimism bias, groupthink, availability, HiPPO effect, watermelon reporting, and pre-mortems are widely used terms in risk, management, and project practice. They are described here in plain language as practitioner concepts, without attributing statistics or study findings to them.
