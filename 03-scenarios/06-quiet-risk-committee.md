# Scenario 6 · The Quiet Risk Committee

> **Illustrative scenario.** Fictional organization and roles.

## Setup
A company's operational risk committee meets monthly. The chief operating officer chairs it. Over three observed meetings, the agenda lists risk ratings "for approval," the COO typically opens each item with a view, and items are approved quickly. In one meeting, the compliance manager questioned a supplier concentration rating; the COO said "we've discussed this already," and the item was approved. The minutes for every meeting read "discussed and agreed."

## Indicators present
| Indicator | What was seen | Evidence grade |
|---|---|---|
| B1 HiPPO effect | Chair's opening view matched the final outcome on most items observed | Repeated pattern (three meetings) |
| B3 Decision made elsewhere | "We've discussed this already"; no record of the prior discussion | Corroborated (minutes + interview) |
| B4 Rubber-stamp approvals | Most items approved quickly, few questions | Repeated pattern (observation notes) |
| C1 Dissent missing from minutes | Compliance manager's challenge not recorded | Corroborated (observation notes + attendee interview) |

## Risk created or hidden
- The committee's oversight exists on paper, but its ratings reflect one person's view.
- Material challenges are lost from the record, so later reviewers, auditors, or the board see unanimous agreement.
- Members learn that challenge is pointless (future chilling effect).

## What the GRC analyst does
1. **Keeps structured observation notes** across several meetings (speaking order, questions asked, outcome) before drawing any conclusion.
2. **Compares minutes against notes** and, separately, against attendee recollection.
3. **Proposes process changes, not personal feedback**: written pre-reads with independent member views submitted in advance; chair speaks last; a rotating challenger; minutes record material concerns and resolutions.
4. **Raises the minutes accuracy issue** with the committee secretary first, then the chair, factually.
5. **If changes are resisted**, notes it for internal audit's governance review or the committee's own oversight body (see [escalation guide](../05-analyst-toolkit/escalation-decision-guide.md)).

## Controls
- **Preventive:** committee charter requires recorded dissent; independent pre-submission of views; facilitation practices (senior voices last, challenger role).
- **Detective:** periodic effectiveness review of the committee (for example by internal audit); sample minutes against recordings or notes where policy permits.

## Framework anchors
COSO 2013 **Principle 2** (board independence and oversight) and **Principle 1** (integrity and ethical values); COSO ERM 2017 **Principle 3** (defines desired culture); NIST CSF 2.0 **GV.OV-01** (strategy outcomes reviewed to adjust direction); IIA Three Lines Model (governing body oversight and internal audit independence).
