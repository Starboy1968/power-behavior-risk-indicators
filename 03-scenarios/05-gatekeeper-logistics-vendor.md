# Scenario 5 · The Gatekeeper

> **Illustrative scenario.** Fictional organization and roles.

## Setup
A regional food distributor relies on a single third-party logistics (3PL) provider for cold-chain transport. The relationship is managed by a long-tenured operations manager who negotiated the original contract and is personally close to the provider's account team. The manager is known internally as "the person who makes it work." The vendor risk team's annual review requests go to this manager, who returns summaries rather than the provider's actual documents. Colleagues say formal approvers "always check with them first." The relationship is informally described as "protected" because a senior executive sponsored the original deal.

## Indicators present
| Indicator | What was seen | Evidence grade |
|---|---|---|
| D1 Single relationship owner | No other employee has a direct contact at the provider | Corroborated (email routing + interviews) |
| D2 Real approval path differs | Approvers wait for the manager's view before signing | Repeated pattern (four of six interviewees) |
| D3 Untouchable sponsor | "That's the sponsor's deal, nobody touches it" | Repeated pattern (interviews) |
| C4 Off-record concerns | Two interviewees mentioned late temperature logs, both asked not to be quoted | Repeated pattern, single source type |

## Risk created or hidden
- Key-person risk: if the manager leaves, institutional knowledge and the relationship go too.
- Information gatekeeping: due diligence sees a filtered view; temperature-control issues may be under-reported (food safety and contractual risk).
- Concentration risk: no qualified alternate provider.
- Possible undisclosed conflict of interest.

## What the GRC analyst does
1. **Requests primary documents directly** from the provider (certifications, temperature-log exceptions, insurance), copying the relationship owner.
2. **Reports interview themes in aggregate**, without attributing the off-record comments, and tests them against data (delivery temperature records, customer complaints).
3. **Maps the formal vs. observed approval path** (see [influence map](../02-framework/influence-map.md)) and presents the difference neutrally.
4. **Confirms conflict-of-interest declarations** are current for the relationship owner and approvers.
5. **Recommends resilience, not blame**: a secondary owner, centralized records, and an exit or alternate-provider plan.

## Controls
- **Preventive:** at least two internal owners for critical suppliers; due diligence evidence obtained directly from the supplier; documented exit plan for critical single-source providers.
- **Detective:** periodic independent performance review (for example quarterly business review attended by procurement or risk); monitoring of supplier-reported exceptions against internal receipt data.
